# Gemtek XR1710G

The XR1710G is the W1700K's design under another label: the same AN7581 and
MT7996, on the same wiring, with the same flash layout and the same stock
bootloader. This is the record of what was read on one, and of the little in
which it differs. The constants are in
[`include/hw/an7581/xr1710g_board.h`](../../include/hw/an7581/xr1710g_board.h);
the board's entry in the build is in [`platform/boards.py`](../../platform/boards.py).

Every line carries where it comes from:

- **[measured]** on one unit (HW label 1.0, board made 2024 week 5), from its
  stock boot log, a backup and a raw image of its NAND, its stock device tree
  and a shell in stock's failsafe mode.
- **[photo]** a marking or a part read from the board, in hand or in the FCC
  filing's photographs.
- **[static]** from a document or another source tree, which is named.

Nothing here is a unit's own: no serial number, MAC address, calibration or
credential.

## What differs from the W1700K

| | W1700K / W1700K2 | XR1710G | |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| GPS receiver | Airoha AG3352, powered through GPIO 43 | none; GPIO 43 is a port LED | [photo], [measured] |
| 1 GbE port LEDs | GPIO 9, 10, 27, 28 | GPIO 33, 34 (yellow), 43, 44 (green), lit when low | [measured] |
| Hardware-revision straps | major on GPIO 30/18, minor on GPIO 45/44 | major reads 1; no minor, GPIO 44 being an LED | [measured] |
| 10 GbE PHYs | RTL8261N (W1700K), RTL8261CE (W1700K2) | RTL8261BE, which answer with the RTL8261N's ID | [photo], [measured] |
| DRAM | two ESMT M16U8G16512A | marked 3CR75 D88PK; the same 2 GiB | [photo], [measured] |
| Stock U-Boot | build AXON 2.3 on the W1700K2 first run on | build AXON 1.6 | [measured] |
| Case | T8H screw; board on the lid | T10H screw under the seal; board on the base | [photo] |

That is all of it, and none of it needs an image of its own: one set of images
runs on both boards.

- **The unit is told which board it is at every boot.** The loader reads the
  serial number from the factory identity, in the `calibration` partition,
  which neither it nor Linux can write. A serial beginning `XR1710G` is this
  board; one beginning `W1700K` is a W1700K or W1700K2. The name (`xr1710g`,
  `w1700k`, or `unknown`) goes to the system in the device tree,
  `/chosen/w1700k,board`, and on the kernel command line, `w1700k.board=`.
- **GPIO 43.** The loader drives it low first of all on every board, since a
  unit it cannot name must be taken for one with a GPS receiver to hold off.
  Here that lights a port LED for a moment; once the unit is named an XR1710G
  the loader drives the line high again, which is how stock holds that LED
  when its port has no link.
- **`w1700k-gps`** does nothing on this board and says it has no receiver.
- **Nothing else knows the difference**: the toolchain, the kernel and its
  device tree, the driver, the root, the radio firmware, the host, and the
  dumper and restorer that stock U-Boot boots from RAM are the W1700K's. The
  dumper that backed this unit up was built before the board was known.

The board monitor answers as the NCT7802 driver expects (vendor `0x50`, chip
`0xc3`, version `0x2x`), as the W1700K's does. Idle in stock's failsafe mode
with the lid closed: 27 °C on the chip, 26 °C on its first remote sensor, the
second remote sensor unconnected, the fan at 1952 RPM on half drive
[measured].

No GPS receiver and no Bluetooth are on the board [photo], and stock's root
has no program, firmware or configuration for either [measured].

## Stock boot chain

- **First stage** trains the DRAM and prints `Secure key exist` twice.
- **Stock U-Boot** is `U-Boot 2014.04-rc1 (Mar 15 2024) AXON 1.6`. Its prompt is
  `ECNT>`, it counts down four seconds before it boots
  (`Hit any key to stop autoboot`), and its `bootcmd` is
  `flash imgread 2048;bootm`. It has `printenv`, `loadb`, `crc32`, `iminfo` and
  `bootm`, which is every command the installer gives it. It boots an unsigned
  FIT staged in RAM, checking its SHA-1 hashes only [measured]. The secure key
  the first stage reports, and that stock Linux confirms ("The key exists in
  this device"), does not stand in the way of that.
- **Stock Linux** is 5.4.55, firmware `XR1710G_fw_5.1.1.12`. From the first
  byte on the console: U-Boot at 4.6 s, the countdown over at 8.8 s, the kernel
  started at 17 s, the firmware's version banner at 91 s [measured].

Two things about stock Linux that matter at the bench [measured]:

- **It gives no login on the console.** A root shell comes from its failsafe
  mode: 43 s after power-on it prints `Press the [f] key and hit [enter] to
  enter failsafe mode`, and `f` and Enter then give a shell with nothing
  mounted for writing.
- **A normal boot changes the unit.** About 50 s in, "Rogue file cleanup"
  deletes files from the settings area (mesh configuration, DHCP reservations,
  backups of the password files and others) and restores three first-run
  scripts. A unit that has booted stock once is no longer as it arrived. To
  keep a unit as received, stop it at U-Boot on its first power-on and back it
  up before stock Linux runs; `init/install --backup-only` does exactly that.

## Flash

Everything this project depends on is the W1700K's [measured]:

- Stock's partition string is
  `2m[bootloader],2m[uenv],2m[dsd],64m[tclinux],64m[tclinux_slave],312m[system]`.
- The vendor bad-block pool is 250 blocks; the BBT is on block 3846 and the BMT
  on block 4095. The unit read had no factory-bad block and no remapped one.
- The identity block is at `0x400000`, and the MT7996 calibration 16 KiB at
  `0x5000` into it, beginning `90 79`, with both crystal trim bytes set.
- The second firmware slot holds a copy of the first slot's image, identical
  over its 41.5 MiB. Stock Linux lists that slot's kernel as `0x2100` bytes all
  the same; that is its bookkeeping, not the flash.

The identity block holds `serial_number`, `manufacturer`, `wan_mac`, `lan_mac`,
`fan_id`, `admin_username`, `admin_password`, `wifi_ssid`, `wifi_passphrase`,
`wifi_guest_passphrase` and `wifi_wps_pin`, so a backup holds the unit's
credentials. Serial numbers begin `XR1710G`. The calibration's own MAC fields
carry MediaTek's prefix `00:0c:43`, not the unit's.

A raw read of the whole chip differed from the corrected one in 148 single
bits, never two in one 512-byte ECC step, against an ECC that corrects eight.

## Wiring

GPIO, all [measured] from stock's device tree and its kernel's table of the
lines it holds; every LED lights when its line is low:

| GPIO | What |
| --- | --- |
| 0 | reset button, active-low |
| 11 | board monitor's alert, idle high [static: stock's fan test script] |
| 17, 19, 20, 29 | status LED: green, blue, white, red |
| 31 | reset of the 10 GbE PHY at MDIO 8, active-low |
| 33, 34 | yellow LEDs of the two 1 GbE ports |
| 43, 44 | green LEDs of the two 1 GbE ports |
| 46 | reset of the 10 GbE PHY at MDIO 5, active-low |

Read as stock leaves the pads: GPIO 30 = 0, GPIO 18 = 1, GPIO 45 = 1. By the
W1700K's formula the first two are major revision 1, the label's "HW 1.0".

PCIe [measured, from the configuration space under stock Linux]: the MT7996's
main interface on PCIe0 at Gen3 on two lanes, window at `0x20000000`; its
second interface on PCIe2 at Gen2 on one lane, window at `0x28000000`; PCIe1
unused. ASPM is off on both links under stock.

Ethernet: the 1 GbE port the installer uses, `lan3`, is switch port 1
[measured: it linked at 1 Gbit/s there]. Stock drives both 10 GbE PHYs with its
RTL8261N driver [measured].

Not yet seen on a unit, and taken from a community tree for this board
[static: naoki66's `an7581-xr1710g.dts`]: that the PHY at MDIO 8 is the jack
called `wan` and the one at MDIO 5 the jack called `lan2`; that the second
1 GbE port is switch port 2; and that both PHYs' lanes are inverted both ways,
as on the W1700K.

## The case, the console and the antennas

All [photo] or seen on the unit in hand.

- **Opening.** One T10H security-Torx screw under the warranty seal by the EEE
  mark, then clips all round the lid. The board is screwed to the base, so
  lifting the lid shows the solder side; the heatsink, the fan, the antenna
  connectors and the console header face the base grille. Eight Phillips
  screws hold the board to the base.
- **Console.** A populated 5-pin header on the component side, beside the DC
  jack and the reset button: 3.3 V, 115200 8N1.
- **Power.** 12 V, 5 A, centre-positive. The FCC filing lists two adapters,
  Lucent Trans 1A98-LJHL and LEI ML60-4120500-A1.
- **Antenna connectors**, all u.FL: CONG5 (DFS and scan), CONA1 to CONA4
  (2.4 and 5 GHz, chains 0 to 3, along the rear edge), J15 to J18 (6 GHz,
  chains 0 to 3, down the side) and J19 (a fifth 6 GHz input, receive only,
  with its own low-noise amplifier).
- **Handling.** The antenna leads are 1.13 mm coax, routed through sheet-metal
  clips to stamped elements on the case walls, and they cross the lid seam and
  the board edge. Open the case flat, lift the lid straight up, lift the board
  by the heatsink and never by a cable, and put every lead back in its clip
  before closing. A pulled lead takes the board's connector with it.

The case is badged *Wi-Fi 6 CERTIFIED* while the FCC grant covers 6 GHz at
320 MHz. That is the certification programme the seller chose; the silicon is
as listed above.

## Building and installing for it

As for a W1700K: `make`, then `init/install`. There is nothing to select. The
installed unit reports its board:

```sh
cat /proc/device-tree/chosen/w1700k,board      # xr1710g
```

## Under this tree

Installed on the unit with `init/install` over the serial transport, from
stock, with no step by hand [measured]:

- **The loader names the board.** It read `serial_number` out of the factory
  identity page, erased flash after it and all, and the system came up with
  `/chosen/w1700k,board` = `xr1710g` and `w1700k.board=xr1710g` on its command
  line. GPIO 43 reads high afterwards: the port LED the loader lit with
  `gps_off` is out again. `w1700k-gps` answers that the board has no receiver.
- **Stock U-Boot AXON 1.6 does everything the installer asks**: `flash erase`,
  `flash write` and `flash read` on the loader slot, `cmp.b`, `saveenv`, and
  `loadb` for both images, with no packet sent twice.
- **Both of the MT7996's interfaces link**: `14c3:7990` at 8 GT/s on two lanes
  and `14c3:7991` at 5 GT/s on one, as on the W1700K, although stock's
  environment here has `serdes_wifi2=01` where a W1700K's has `00`. The driver
  binds and turns ASPM off.
- **The 10 GbE PHYs bind to the kernel's `RTL8261BE 10Gbps PHY` driver**, at
  MDIO 5 and 8, with the built-in `rtl8261n.bin`.
- **The board monitor binds to `nct7802`** and reads 24.6 °C and 1971 RPM.
- **The radio comes up under our firmware.** `w1700k load` takes 2.5 s, from
  the unit's own calibration, read out of its partition: all three bands
  ready, all three synthesizers locked, no bring-up failure counted. The
  synthesizers lock over 2070.0 to 2734.6 MHz on band 0, 4672.5 to 7561.3 MHz
  on band 1 and 4700.5 to 7585.9 MHz on band 2 (the W1700K measured for
  `host/w1700k/README.md`: 2080.7 to 2748.0, 4646.6 to 7509.0, 4707.2 to
  7576.2).
- **The 10 GbE `wan` jack is the PHY at MDIO 8**, `eth1`, and carries data both
  ways: against an RTL8159 USB adapter it linked at 5 Gbit/s 17 s after the
  adapter appeared, and 8200-byte frames pass. So the lane polarity taken from
  the W1700K is right for this PHY at least.
- **It streams.** Band 1 at 40 Msps, raw, over that link for two minutes:
  40.006 Msps, 1283 Mbit/s, no slice lost, late or damaged. `tools/unit_sweep.py`
  on the same link: 28 of 30 modes pass, among them every band at 40, 80, 160
  and 320 Msps and the 6 GHz 640/2 capture. The two that fail are the 16-bit
  reduced packing at 40 Msps (0.3 to 0.9 % of slices lost, three runs) and at
  80 Msps (0.17 to 0.35 %, failing one run in three); the same packing at
  160 Msps loses nothing.
- **Those two were the host, not the board, and are cured there.** Read again
  with every counter taken on both ends: the unit's senders sent every slice
  and its port dropped nothing, while the host's RTL8159 adapter counted the
  datagrams that did not arrive, one for one. The host, a tablet, went into its
  deepest idle state between the clumps a slow stream arrives in, and woke too
  slowly for the adapter; any one of its CPUs kept awake cured it.
  `w1700k-stream` now asks the kernel to keep the CPUs out of deep idle while
  datagrams arrive, and `init/host/link` finds the interrupt of a USB
  controller that is no PCI device, which it had not moved on this host. With
  both the sweep is 30 of 30 with no slice lost in any mode, and 320 Msps lost
  2 slices of 18.8 million in two minutes
  ([the adapter's notes](../../tools/r8152-rtl8159/README.md)).
- **The flash layout attaches**: the vendor tables translate (pool 250, no bad
  block), `system` and `durable` hold what was written, and `lan3` links at
  1 Gbit/s.
